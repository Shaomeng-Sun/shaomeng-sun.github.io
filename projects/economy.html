Project Overview

Game economies connect resource generation, spending, progression, rewards, and player engagement. This project presents a framework for modeling these relationships and evaluating alternative economy designs through simulation.

The goal is to support data-informed decisions about progression pacing, resource availability, reward structures, and long-term economic balance.

Problem & Objectives

How can an in-game economy remain balanced as players progress?

An economy must provide a satisfying sense of progression without creating excessive resource accumulation, frustrating bottlenecks, or unsustainable reward dynamics. Simulation can help evaluate these trade-offs before implementing changes in a live game.

  • Understand resource inflows and outflows over time.
  • Evaluate progression speed and potential bottlenecks.
  • Compare alternative reward and cost configurations.
  • Identify potential inflation, scarcity, or resource imbalances.
  • Support economy design decisions with quantitative evidence.

Design Recommendations

  1. Balance resource inflows and outflows. Use model outputs to identify configurations that could produce persistent surpluses or shortages.
  2. Evaluate progression pacing. Compare the time required to reach key milestones under different reward and cost configurations.
  3. Test changes before implementation. Use simulations to narrow down promising configurations before validating them against actual player behavior.
  4. Validate predictions with live data. Compare simulated outcomes with observed telemetry and refine the model when the two diverge.

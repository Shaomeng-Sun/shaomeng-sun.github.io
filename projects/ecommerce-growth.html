Ecommerce Growth & Conversion Analysis

Where does ecommerce revenue come from, where do recorded journeys break down, and how much can we trust the underlying measurement?

Executive summary

Business insight depends on measurement quality.

Using a public GA4 ecommerce sample, I built and validated a SQL-based analytical pipeline to investigate conversion activity, resolved transactions, product revenue and recorded traffic-source segments. The results identify revenue concentration in Apparel and show that missing tracking events and purchase identifiers materially constrain funnel and late-January revenue interpretation.

This is a descriptive portfolio case study. No live business changes, incremental revenue gains or causal conversion improvements are claimed.

Key findings

Three findings that shape the business recommendations

47.68%

Apparel dominates recorded item revenue

Apparel generated $160,774 from 371 purchased product IDs. Revenue concentration makes it a strong candidate for deeper assortment-level investigation, but revenue alone is not profitability.

2,046

Purchasing sessions lack complete recorded paths

Of 4,848 sessions with a purchase event, only 2,802 contain all five funnel stages in chronological order. Missing cart tracking on 18 dates contributes to this gap.

89.42%

Late-January purchase identifiers deteriorate

During January 29–31, 93 of 104 raw purchase events lacked a transaction-ID parameter. This makes the recorded revenue decline unsafe to interpret as an equivalent demand decline.

Conversion analysis

A recorded funnel is not the same as total purchases

The sequential funnel requires each recorded stage to occur at or after the previous stage within the same session. This stricter definition exposes missing or out-of-order events rather than proving customers abandoned an actual step.

Measurement reliability

Why a revenue decline needs a quality check first

Resolved transaction revenue was $157,575 in December and $56,410 in January. The decline is visible in the reconciled dataset, but missing transaction IDs increased during January, so the observed change cannot be assumed to represent the full change in actual purchasing activity.

Created staging, intermediate and analytical marts for sessions, chronological funnel paths, resolved transactions, product performance and daily quality measures.

Dataset: Google Analytics BigQuery ecommerce sample dataset. Analysis performed with BigQuery SQL and Looker Studio.

Recommendations

What I would prioritize next

  1. Fix purchase identifiers first. Investigate the late-January transaction-ID parameter loss and restore reliable transaction reconciliation before evaluating the revenue trend.
  2. Improve funnel instrumentation. Audit missing add-to-cart events and verify chronological event capture before using stage drop-off to prioritize optimization experiments.
  3. Deepen product analysis. Examine leading Apparel and Bags products alongside margin, inventory and verified catalog attributes before assortment decisions.
  4. Strengthen source attribution. Review source assignment, obfuscated labels and self-referrals before reallocating acquisition spend.

What this project demonstrates

End-to-end SQL analytics, session and transaction modeling, data-quality controls, dashboard design, and business communication under imperfect measurement. The case study separates observed associations from causal or commercial claims that the data cannot establish.

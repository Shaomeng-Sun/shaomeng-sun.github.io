A/B Testing Analytics

Designed and analyzed controlled experiments to evaluate the impact of different reminder strategies on parcel pickup behavior.

Python SQL A/B Testing Statistics Causal Analysis

Project Overview

This project analyzes an A/B test comparing different customer reminder strategies and their effect on parcel pickup behavior. The analysis focuses on experiment validity, data quality, treatment differences, and actionable recommendations.

Business Question

How can reminder strategies improve pickup speed?

The objective was to determine whether SMS, email, or a combination of channels could encourage customers to pick up parcels faster while providing a reliable basis for future experimentation.

Experiment Design

Customers were assigned to different reminder strategies:

SMS Only

Customers received SMS reminders according to the experimental reminder schedule.

SMS + Email

Customers received both SMS and email reminders.

Default Reminder

Customers received the existing reminder strategy.

Email Only

Customers received email reminders without SMS.

No Reminder

Customers received no reminder intervention and served as a comparison group.

Data & Key Metrics

Primary Metric

Pickup time after the first reminder.

Operational Metrics

Number of reminders sent and time spent in the locker.

Quality Checks

Treatment assignment, timestamps, duplicates, and reminder consistency.

Data Quality & Validation

Before comparing treatment groups, I performed data-quality checks to identify inconsistencies that could bias the experiment analysis.

  • Checked whether customers were consistently assigned to a single experimental group.
  • Investigated cases where observed reminder counts did not match the expected reminder schedule.
  • Identified inconsistent SMS counts across experimental groups.
  • Checked timestamp relationships between locker visits, reminders, and parcel pickup.
  • Investigated duplicate orders and conflicting treatment assignments.

Analytical Approach

01

Validate the Experiment

Checked treatment assignment, sample sizes, duplicates, and data consistency before analyzing outcomes.

02

Compared pickup-time distributions and pickup performance across reminder strategies.

03

Quantify Differences

Estimated differences between treatment groups using appropriate statistical comparisons.

04

Translate Results

Converted the statistical findings into practical recommendations for the next experiment.

Key Results

Observed Pattern

Reminder strategies affected pickup behavior

The experimental groups showed meaningful differences in pickup performance, indicating that reminder design can influence customer behavior.

Incremental Effect

Email provided an additional uplift

The analysis estimated an incremental improvement from adding email to SMS, which motivated further validation in a controlled experiment.

Data Insight

Data quality was critical

Inconsistent reminder counts and timestamps highlighted the importance of validating experiment instrumentation before drawing conclusions.

Recommendations

  1. Run a focused follow-up experiment. Compare SMS + email, SMS only, and the current default strategy to isolate the incremental value of email.
  2. Improve experiment instrumentation. Ensure reminder counts, timestamps, and treatment assignments are consistently recorded.
  3. Test timing and message content. Once the reminder strategy is validated, optimize when reminders are sent and what they communicate.
  4. Retain a control group. Maintain a holdout group during rollout so the impact of the chosen strategy can continue to be measured.
